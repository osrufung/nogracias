The 400 prefix

What it is, since when it's mandatory and what you can do if they keep calling.

What it is

400 is a range of nine-digit numbers that Spain's National Telephone Numbering Plan reserves for sales calls. It was approved by the Resolution of 14 April 2026 of the Secretary of State for Telecommunications and Digital Infrastructure, published in the BOE (Official State Gazette) on 16 April 2026. Its purpose is to let you know a call is a sales call before you answer.

The resolution builds on Law 10/2025 on customer service, which calls for sales calls to be clearly told apart from customer service calls. (The official texts are in Spanish.)

Since when

Since 17 October 2026, sales calls must come from 400 numbers, and carriers block sales calls that use other numbers, such as mobile or landline numbers.

Good to know

  • They're outgoing-only numbers: you can be called from a 400 number, but you can't call it back.
  • Customer service doesn't change: service calls from a company you're a customer of don't have to use 400.
  • There are gaps: call centres outside Spain, or anyone breaking the rules, can still call from other numbers.

What you can do

1. Filter 400 calls

With «No, gracias» you identify or block them on your iPhone. Some carriers also offer to block the whole 400 range on request: ask yours.

2. Join the Robinson List

The Robinson List is Spain's free advertising opt-out service. Companies running sales campaigns must check it and not call people on it, unless you've given them your consent.

3. Ask the company to stop calling

You can exercise your right to object: ask the company to stop using your data for marketing. Saying so during the call is enough, or you can write to their customer service or data protection officer.

4. File a complaint

If a company keeps calling after you've objected, or calls you without consent while you're on the Robinson List, you can file a complaint with the Spanish Data Protection Agency (AEPD). Note the number, date and time of each call.
